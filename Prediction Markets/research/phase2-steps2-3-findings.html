Edge Verification · Capacity & Fees

What the real order books say about a $50 weather/macro edge

I pulled live and settled markets straight from Kalshi's public API and computed spreads, fees, capacity, overround, and realized outcomes. The headline: at $50, the edge is killed or saved at the level of fees and spreads — and weather is structurally the cheapest, highest-frequency place to find it.

⚠️
Scope & honesty. This is a real but deliberately small probe: 2 live NYC weather events (12 buckets), 1 live jobless-claims event (10 buckets), and 6 settled NYC weather days (36 markets) — the sample one public-API pass returns. It is enough to quantify microstructure (spreads, fees, overround, capacity, frequency) with confidence, and to establish the method for the forecast-calibration test. It is not a finished calibration study: that requires fixed-lead-time price history across many days and is the toolkit's MS2 backtest job (protocol specified at the end). Not financial advice.

Kalshi quadratic fee ≈ ⌈0.07·C·p·(1−p)⌉. As a taker you hand back 4–20% of every dollar — more than any realistic forecast edge. As a maker on weather it's zero. Maker-only isn't a preference; it's survival.

Capacity is not the limit

Top-of-book on liquid weather buckets ranges from a few dollars to $13+; jobless buckets show $14–$4,400 at best. A $50 bankroll spread across buckets and days fits comfortably inside available depth. The constraint at this size is fee-adjusted edge, not whether you can get filled.

Weather offers ~25–35× more shots

  • Weather: ~6 buckets/day/city → ~180/mo for NYC alone; ~1,000–1,400/mo across the 8 confirmed high-temp cities.
  • Jobless: weekly → ~40 markets/mo. CPI/NFP/Fed: ~monthly.
  • More independent resolutions = faster calibration and a quicker, cheaper read on whether the model works.
Step 3 verdict

For a $50 bankroll: trade weather, maker-only. Its 1¢ spreads + zero maker fees + high frequency make it the only category where a small edge survives costs and where you get enough resolutions to learn fast. Jobless is viable but wider-spread and ~25× lower frequency → secondary. Crossing the spread as a taker anywhere is a structural loser at this size.

There's a structural vig to be on the right side of

Summing the mid-implied probabilities across a weather event's buckets gives 1.04–1.05 — a +4–5% overround. Translation: the book is tilted ~4–5% against takers and toward a disciplined maker. Combined with zero maker fees, posting liquidity is how you sit on the right side of that tilt instead of paying it.

The longshot tail is a pure tax

Every deep-tail weather bucket we sampled quotes bid 0¢ / ask 1¢ — you can only buy a near-0% outcome at 1¢. That's the favorite-longshot bias made concrete: buying tails is −EV, and the edge sits in fading over-priced tails / favoring under-priced near-favorites, exactly as the Phase-1 research predicted.

The key methodological finding: settled prices converge to truth

Across 36 settled NYC markets, every price had collapsed to 0 or 1 by settlement — 30 at ≤5¢, 6 at ≥95¢, none in between:

Settled at ≤ 5¢ (losers)

This is why you cannot measure forecast edge from settled prices — by settlement the answer is known and priced. The real calibration/Brier/“1.27× over-priced uncertainty” test must use the price at a fixed lead time before resolution (e.g. 9:00 AM ET on event day) versus the realized outcome. That requires candlestick history per market — precisely the toolkit's MS2 backtest.

🔬
On the 1.27× uncertainty claim: our sample shows implied single-day SD ≈ 1.7°F and realized cross-day SD ≈ 2.85°F — but these measure different things (one day's forecast width vs. variation across the season) and n=6 days is far too small to conclude anything. The honest test is per-day implied SD vs. that day's realized error, over months. Specified below for the harness.
Step 2 verdict

The structural edges are real and measurable right now: a +4–5% overround and a quantifiable longshot tail tax that a maker can systematically sit on the right side of. The forecast edge (is your weather model better than the market's implied distribution?) is real in the literature but can only be proven with fixed-lead price history — so it graduates to the toolkit/simulator, not a hand analysis.

Exact calibration protocol for the toolkit (MS2 / sim PS4)

  • For each weather city-day, capture the full bucket ladder at a fixed lead (e.g. 9:00 AM ET event day) via /markets/{ticker}/candlesticks; normalize out the overround to get implied bucket probabilities.
  • Generate the model bucket probabilities from the Open-Meteo GFS/ECMWF ensemble for that city-day (same lead).
  • Join to the realized NWS high (winning bucket). Compute, per day and per city: Brier score (model vs market), reliability curve, and the implied-SD ÷ realized-error ratio — the real test of the 1.27× claim.
  • Score only maker-fillable, fee-adjusted edges (apply the §6.6 fee model; weather maker = 0). Require net edge ≥ 2× costs.
  • Run ≥3–4 weeks live (the simulator's forward-test) before any real-money pilot. Decision gate: fund only if the model is provably calibrated and fee-adjusted-positive over the window.

Net: Steps 2–3 confirm the structural case for weather-first, maker-only at $50, and convert the remaining open question into one precise, runnable measurement. That measurement is the simulator's whole purpose — so the natural next move is to build it (specs already written) and let it run the protocol above.
