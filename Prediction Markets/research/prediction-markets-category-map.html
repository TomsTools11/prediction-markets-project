The Thesis

The edge lives where outcomes resolve on public data — not where the crowd is loudest

Sports is ~85–90% of Kalshi's volume, but volume is not opportunity. For an AI/model approach on a tiny bankroll, the best categories are the ones that are cleanly modelable from structured public data, demonstrably mispriced, and contested by patient forecasters rather than co-located bots. Two categories dominate on that test.

Bottom line

Lead with Kalshi (it has a full trading API + demo sandbox; Polymarket's US app is iOS-only with no automation path yet). Focus the first models on two categories where forecasting skill — not speed or capital — is the differentiator:

①Weather — Daily Temperature ②Macro / Economic Data ③Politics (test)

Keep Polymarket as a secondary venue for liquidity, broader event coverage, and — later, with automation — cross-platform arbitrage. Treat sports, short-horizon crypto, and pop-culture as watch/avoid for now (reasons below).

Why Kalshi is the primary venue

A model is only useful if it can read prices and post orders programmatically. Kalshi exposes a full API with a demo environment for risk-free testing, plus a Python SDK — and it owns the two most model-friendly categories (NWS-settled weather, government-data-settled macro). That combination is exactly what an AI-signal workflow needs.

Why keep Polymarket in the picture

Deeper liquidity on flagship markets, a broader long-tail of event types, and the most active AI-agent ecosystem. It's the other leg of any future cross-platform arbitrage, and a useful price-discovery reference. But until web/API access lands, it's a manual venue — a secondary tool, not where a bot lives.

Scoring Framework

Six criteria that decide whether AI can win a category

A category is "optimizable" for our profile only if a model can be built, the edge is real, and the game isn't won purely on speed or size. Each category is scored 1–5 on these, then weighted for a small, patient, automated trader.

① Modelability · 25%

Does the outcome resolve on clean, structured, public data you can forecast (NWS temps, CPI prints) — or on something noisy and idiosyncratic?

② Documented edge · 20%

Is there observable, repeatable mispricing (e.g. weather's 1.27× over-priced uncertainty, favorite-longshot bias) you can point to?

③ Skill > speed · 20%

Does a better forecast win — or is it a latency war against co-located bots? We want categories where patience beats microseconds.

④ Small-capital fit · 15%

Can a tiny, patient maker actually get filled without being the dumb money or getting crowded out by sharps?

⑤ Automatability · 10%

Are the price feed and the signal data both reachable by API, so the whole loop can run as code?

⑥ Fee-adjusted viability · 10%

Does the typical edge survive fees at small size? (On Kalshi, that usually means posting limit orders, not crossing the spread.)

Category Deep Dives

The case on each, in detail

Weather — Daily Temperature (Kalshi KXHIGH)

Focus · 94

The cleanest AI play on the board. Contracts ask whether a city's daily high lands in a temperature bucket, and they settle on the official National Weather Service report — a single, objective, public number. That makes the outcome forecastable with the same numerical weather models (GFS, ECMWF) and ensemble methods that meteorologists already use.

The documented inefficiency is specific and exploitable: across ~1,900 city-date observations, Kalshi's markets priced ~1.27× more temperature uncertainty than actually occurred — i.e., the wings (unlikely buckets) are systematically over-priced. A calibrated model that buys the under-priced likely bucket and fades the over-priced tails has a structural reason to win, and open-source implementations (31-member GFS ensembles from Open-Meteo + Kelly sizing) already exist as reference.

Resolves on
NWS / NWS climate report (next-morning)
Signal inputs
GFS & ECMWF ensembles, METAR airport obs, climatology
Edge type
Mispriced forecast uncertainty (1.27×) + model blend
Why <$500 works
Thin markets — small, patient maker orders fit perfectly
✅
Verdict — build first. Highest modelability, a concrete documented edge, fully API-automatable, and thin enough that a small bankroll is an advantage, not a handicap. The main watch-outs: the wings near 50¢ carry the heaviest fees (trade where edge is largest), and bot competition here is rising, so the calibration advantage has a shelf life.

Macro / Economic Data (Kalshi — CPI, Fed funds, jobs, GDP)

Focus · 83

The other category that resolves on a scheduled public number: a BLS inflation print, a BEA GDP release, an FOMC decision. A Federal Reserve working paper and NBER research found Kalshi's macro markets match or beat traditional forecasters — its CPI estimates carried ~40% lower error than consensus (and up to ~67% lower on surprise prints). That validates the markets as accurate, which cuts both ways: the edge isn't "the market is dumb," it's "can your nowcast beat the marginal trader's between releases."

The opening is the gap between scheduled data and live pricing. Public nowcasting tools (Cleveland Fed inflation nowcast, real-time CPI trackers, the FOMC calendar, fed-funds futures) let you form a sharper probability than a market that's drifting on stale information — then position ahead of the release. It's a research-and-reaction game, far less of a latency war than crypto.

Edge type

Verdict — build alongside weather. Slightly harder edge than weather (the market is already good), but the data is just as clean, it's fully automatable, and it's intellectually in your wheelhouse. Pair a public nowcast with a calibration model and measure whether you can beat the market's pre-release price.

Politics & Elections

Test · 71

The richest behavioral edge — and the messiest data. Studies consistently find a favorite-longshot bias and price compression toward 50% at long horizons, especially in political markets where opposing partisan bets cancel out. Translation: favorites are often under-priced and longshots over-priced, a pattern you can lean on systematically. Liquidity is excellent in-cycle (and Polymarket led price discovery in the 2024 cycle).

The catch: outcomes don't resolve on a tidy data feed, signals are partly unstructured (polls, news, sentiment), and there's real insider/asymmetric-information risk — the CFTC framework permits trading on non-public information in many cases. This is where an LLM layer earns its keep (parsing polling shifts and news), but it's a test, not a core build.

Sports

Watch · 57

Where all the volume is, and the worst place for a newcomer's edge. Sports is ~85–90% of Kalshi volume precisely because it's contested by sharp sportsbooks and pro traders. Top books' AI models beat the closing line by 3–7%, so prediction-market prices on major events are already efficient — what models "know" is priced in. Realistic edges exist only in (a) thin sub-leagues (MLS, college) at 3–5%, or (b) fast arbitrage vs. DraftKings/FanDuel — and arbitrage is a speed-and-capital game.

Culture & Entertainment (awards, box office, pop culture)

Watch · 55

Genuinely inefficient — thin markets, casual "dumb money," wide spreads. But inefficiency cuts both ways: low liquidity makes prices easy to manipulate, capacity is tiny, signals are idiosyncratic and hard to automate, and the insider-information risk is acute (people close to a release, a studio, or a vote can trade on it legally here). A few corners — box-office openings, awards with public precedent — are partly modelable, but most is noise.

Crypto — Short-Horizon Price (15-min BTC/ETH/SOL)

Avoid · 44

Looks AI-friendly, is actually a trap for retail. The eye-catching wins here — one wallet reportedly turning ~$300 into ~$400k in a month — came from execution latency, not forecasting: bots arbitraging the gap between a prediction market's order book and live Binance/Coinbase prices, reacting in milliseconds. On Polymarket, 14 of the 20 most profitable wallets are bots and AI agents are 30%+ of activity, but they win on speed and infrastructure you can't match from a laptop. Short-horizon price is close to a martingale; there's no forecasting edge to model.

AI Signal Stack

How the "AI identifies signals → calculated recommendations" loop actually runs

For the focus categories, the workflow is the same five-stage loop. AI does the forecasting and edge-detection; disciplined rules do the sizing and execution.

01
Ingest

Pull market prices via Kalshi API + signal data (weather ensembles / econ nowcasts) on a schedule.

02
Forecast

Probabilistic model outputs a calibrated probability for each bucket (boosted trees for weather; nowcast blend for macro; LLM for unstructured context).

03
Find edge

Compare your probability to the market price. Flag only gaps that clear fees by a margin.

04
Size

Fractional-Kelly position sizing on the edge, capped hard for a small bankroll.

05
Execute & monitor

Post maker (limit) orders to dodge taker fees; log predicted vs. realized; watch for calibration drift / alpha decay.

The discipline that makes it work

Three rules separate a real edge from a slow bleed: (1) be the maker — at <$500, crossing the spread to take liquidity hands your edge to fees; (2) trust calibration, not vibes — track a Brier score on every market and only deploy where the model is provably calibrated; (3) assume decay — when an edge is documented publicly (as weather's is), more bots arrive, so measure your realized edge continuously and rotate when it compresses.

Reality Check

What can quietly kill the edge

Fees at small size

Kalshi taker fees peak near 50¢ contracts (~$1.75/100). On a tiny bankroll, crossing the spread can erase the entire modeled edge. Maker-only discipline isn't optional.

Alpha decay

Every edge cited here is public. As AI forecasting becomes common, the inefficiencies get arbitraged away — the classic decay problem. A documented edge is a starting hypothesis, not an annuity.

Forecast ≠ execution

Much of the visible "AI wins" on these venues is latency arbitrage, not better prediction. Don't mistake a bot's speed advantage for a model edge you can replicate.

Insider & thin-market risk

The CFTC framework allows trading on non-public info in many cases, and thin markets are manipulable. Both hit politics and culture hardest — another reason to anchor on data-resolved categories.

Platform & legal drift

Polymarket US is iOS-only today and a Minnesota ban is set for Aug 1, 2026 (under challenge). State-level access can change; confirm your eligibility before funding.

Model overfit

Backtests on prediction markets are prone to look-ahead bias and overfitting. Live paper-trading is the only honest validation — build it in before risking capital.

Recommended Next Steps

Phase 2 — pick the single best category, with evidence

Phase 1 narrowed the field to two focus categories and a test. Phase 2 replaces judgment scores with measured, fee-adjusted, paper-traded evidence — and ends at a clear go/no-go.

Step 1

Map the exact markets & settlement

~2–3 days · Kalshi API + docs
  • Catalog the specific Kalshi series for weather (KXHIGH cities) and macro (CPI, Fed funds, payrolls, GDP) — contract specs, buckets, schedules, settlement source.
  • Stand up API access in the demo sandbox and confirm you can read order books and post test orders programmatically.
  • Resolve your platform eligibility (Kalshi state check; whether Polymarket US iOS access is open to you).
Step 2

Verify the edge on historical data

~1 week · data + backtest harness
  • Pull historical prices + realized outcomes; re-test the weather "1.27× over-priced uncertainty" claim yourself across cities.
  • Measure the macro markets' pre-release pricing vs. a public nowcast (Cleveland Fed) to see if a beatable gap exists.
  • Run a calibration study (Brier scores) and quantify favorite-longshot bias on resolved political markets as the "test" category benchmark.
Step 3

Pressure-test capacity & fees

~2–3 days
  • For each candidate market: typical spread, depth at top of book, and whether the modeled edge survives fees as a maker at <$500 size.
  • Estimate realistic monthly trade count and fill rates — does the category produce enough opportunities to matter?
Step 4

Paper-trade live, then decide

~3–4 weeks · shadow run
  • Run the full loop live with no capital: log predicted probability, market price, and realized outcome for every market, daily.
  • Compute hypothetical fee-adjusted P&L, hit rate, and calibration per category — head-to-head, weather vs. macro.
  • Pick the winner on risk-adjusted, fee-adjusted, capacity-aware expected value — then design the live execution + AI signal stack for that one category.
Decision gate: fund the smallest real-money pilot only in the category whose paper-traded, fee-adjusted edge is positive and provably calibrated over the shadow period. If neither clears, iterate the model — don't deploy.
